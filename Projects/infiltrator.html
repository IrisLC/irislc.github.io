Infiltrator, Spring 2025

Unity, Enemy & Level Programmer, Level Designer, Team Co-Lead

What Is This?

A FPS, stealth game developed over the course of the Spring 2025 semester for a class in Indie Game Development. This project blends 2D pixel art and 3D assets to create a unique visual experience for the player.

You can find this game on itch.io here.

What Was My Part?

Worked as the Enemy designer, Level Designer, and was the team lead throughout the latter two thirds of the project among a team of 6.

The Challenges

The primary challenge for Infiltrator was time, the game was developed for a class, so we only had a single semester to make a game demo. Another issue that arose was the fact that our had two 3d artists and one pixel artist, so if we wanted to make the most out of the team members we had, we would have to get creative to allow all three artists their chance to shine. This is not to mention the fact that half of our team had already started their Senior Capstone projects so their time was split between two games, and understandably, Infiltrator was not their top priority. Finally, there were all the challenges that normally arise from game development, only compounded by the lack of experience we all had.

The Results

I used this project as a learning opportunity, trying to hone the skills I had already developed, but also trying to learn as much as I could over the course of development. This project allowed me to expand on my work with Strange Tower to create much more complex enemy AI. I implemented a Finite State Machine based behavior model, to allow much more complex and interesting behaviors from the NPC's in the game. To further this I was able to implement a number of different systems regarding the enemies, including: level wide enemy communication, differences between patrolling enemies and camera systems, a system that splits a single enemy's awareness of the player from the site-wide awareness, a sound system, and an investigation system where an enemy will scan an area around if they find anything suspicious, which depending on how alert they are can range from an open door to a dead body.

To solve the issues of conflicting art mediums I created a script for dynamic sprite billboards, so that we could have 2D enemies walking around a 3D world. For the problem of time and people having different priorities, firstly so that people with limited time or understanding of code could meaningfully contribute to the design of the level, I created a tool to easily create enemy patrol routes. This way the members of the team who were not experienced with code could quickly and easily add or change the path an enemy will take entirely through visual elements. This was my first experience creating developer tools for a project. Also, when the person who was originally our team leader had to take a step back from the project, I stepped up to fill his role to ensure the different parts of our development were moving smoothly.

Finally, this project was also my first foray into Level Design. At some point early on in the project there was a break down in communication between our environmental artist and our level designer, resulting in us having an improperly made level given to us two days before our first showcase. So, alleviate this, I ended up spending ~6 hours creating a brand new level out of the elements I had been provided, which hte final result is the level currently in our game.
